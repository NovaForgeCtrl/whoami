# Philosophy
[README](README.md) · [About Me](about.md) · [Projects](projects.md)

I don't really have a grand philosophy.

Mostly, I just have a few things I've learned about how I like to work.

---

## Understand, Don't Just Use

I don't want to memorize commands just because they make something work.

I want to understand what they actually do.

If I configure something, I want to know what is happening underneath.

If something fails, I want to know why.

---

## Break Things

A system that never breaks doesn't teach you very much.

One of the advantages of having a homelab is being able to experiment.

Things will break.

Configurations will be wrong.

Services will stop working.

And sometimes the solution will turn out to be something ridiculously small.

That's part of learning.

---

## Keep Digging

When something doesn't make sense, I have a hard time just accepting it.

I keep digging.

Sometimes that's useful.

Sometimes I probably spend far too much time on something that could have been left alone.

But the deeper I understand a problem, the more likely I am to remember the solution.

---

## Document It

If I had to figure something out, someone else probably will too.

That's why I document things.

Not because everything I write is perfect.

But because future me will eventually forget how something worked.

And future me is probably going to be very grateful that I wrote it down.

---

## Don't Pretend

I'm still learning.

I don't know everything.

I'm not an expert.

I'm not going to pretend that I am.

I'd rather say "I don't know yet" and then go find out.

---

## Build Something Real

Tutorials are useful.

Courses are useful.

Documentation is essential.

But eventually I want to build something that actually works outside of a tutorial.

That's why I build projects like LUMS.

The goal isn't to prove that I already know everything.

The goal is to find out what I don't know yet.

---

## Why?

I don't just want things to work.

I want to understand **why** they work — and what happens when they don't.

That's probably the closest thing I have to a philosophy.
